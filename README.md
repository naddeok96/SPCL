# Curriculum Mixing Strategy

This project studies how to stage MNIST training data of different difficulty levels inside a curriculum learning loop, and how to automate that loop with reinforcement learning and off-policy optimization. Three core ideas drive the design: (1) always expose the model to deliberate mixtures of easy, medium, and hard subsets, (2) evaluate downstream progress with a metric that treats every subset fairly, and (3) search the curriculum space aggressively before handing it over to an agent for fine-grained control.

## Curriculum data construction
The environment starts every episode by sampling feasible proportions for each subset from the ranges in `config.yaml:4` through `config.yaml:8`. Using those proportions, `curriculum_env.CurriculumEnv.reset` rebuilds the `Subset` objects that point into the easy, medium, and hard MNIST variants and caches new `DataLoader` instances (`curriculum_env.py:152`, `curriculum_env.py:171`). When the agent supplies a mixing ratio during a phase, `curriculum.get_mixed_loader` converts it into `WeightedRandomSampler` weights so that each mini-batch reflects the requested blend irrespective of the absolute subset sizes (`curriculum.py:187`, `curriculum.py:205`). This mechanism keeps the curriculum responsive even as the environment re-samples underlying indices and architectures on every reset (`curriculum_env.py:173`, `curriculum_env.py:190`).

## Macro accuracy as a reward signal
After each phase, the environment measures accuracy on all three subset loaders and averages the results to produce a macro accuracy reward (`curriculum.py:127`, `curriculum.py:133`). That averaging step prevents the policy from overfitting to the dominant subset while starving the others. Episode termination scales the last reward by ten whenever the agent exhausts its phases or sample budget, making complete curricula stand out inside the replay buffer (`curriculum_env.py:214`, `curriculum_env.py:217`). Because macro accuracy aligns directly with the curriculum goal-balanced competence across data difficulties-it is also the metric plotted in the evaluation dashboards that `off_policy_train` renders during checkpoints (`off_policy_train.py:451`, `off_policy_train.py:477`).

## Reinforcement learning formulation
The observation vector concatenates six loss histograms (correct and incorrect buckets for each subset), relative subset sizes, and two scalar progress indicators: normalized phase index and remaining sample fraction (`curriculum_env.py:138`, `curriculum_env.py:149`). These features mirror the decomposition used in the diagnostic plots (`off_policy_train.py:42`, `off_policy_train.py:67`), so the agent consumes the same signals that humans inspect. Actions are five-dimensional: learning rate, three mixture weights, and the fraction of the remaining sample budget to spend this phase. The actor network emits them through sigmoid and softmax heads to guarantee valid ranges (`rl_agent.py:24`, `rl_agent.py:37`), and the environment unpacks the tensor into concrete hyperparameters for `run_phase_training` (`curriculum_env.py:208`, `curriculum_env.py:214`). Each action triggers a short burst of SGD with the mixed loader (`curriculum.py:100`, `curriculum.py:121`), then the next state reflects the updated loss landscape and resource counters. This tight loop means the agent directly observes the consequence of its chosen mixture and step size.

## Evolutionary pretraining of the replay buffer
Before the actor-critic agent begins learning, an evolutionary search explores the curriculum space aggressively. Candidate solutions encode the full sequence of actions for all phases, constrained per phase to valid learning-rate ranges, normalized mixtures, and usage fractions (`generate_dataset.py:71`, `generate_dataset.py:104`). Each candidate runs through a fresh copy of the environment, contributing every transition-state, action, reward, next state, and terminal flag-to the history list that ultimately becomes the replay dataset (`generate_dataset.py:147`, `generate_dataset.py:193`). Mutation and crossover introduce diversity while preserving feasibility (`generate_dataset.py:90`, `generate_dataset.py:123`), and the evolutionary settings themselves (population size, generations, top-k elitism) come straight from the configuration file (`config.yaml:25`, `config.yaml:33`). Because the evolutionary sweep touches a broad range of curricula, the replay buffer starts with meaningful trajectories that already respect the macro accuracy reward shaping.

## Off-policy DDPG optimization
The training script loads the evolutionary dataset, seeds either a uniform replay buffer or a prioritized replay buffer (`off_policy_train.py:248`, `off_policy_train.py:255`, `replay_buffer.py:1`, `replay_buffer.py:74`), and instantiates the DDPG agent with consistent actor and critic targets (`rl_agent.py:80`, `rl_agent.py:96`). During updates the twin critics share a smoothed target generated from the actor target plus clipped Gaussian noise, mirroring standard TD3 practices and keeping Q-value estimates stable (`rl_agent.py:181`, `rl_agent.py:194`). Every `policy_delay` steps the actor optimizes against the first critic while the target networks undergo soft updates controlled by `tau` (`rl_agent.py:233`, `rl_agent.py:247`). Exploration noise is annealed over the scheduled number of updates so the policy transitions from broad searches to precise adjustments (`rl_agent.py:242`, `rl_agent.py:247`). Periodic checkpoints replay the whole curriculum without exploration, capture the macro accuracy trace, loss curves, and action variance, and save model weights for future comparisons (`off_policy_train.py:423`, `off_policy_train.py:489`). This workflow mixes systematic evaluation with the agent's off-policy learning, allowing users to track whether the policy continues to improve on balanced accuracy.

## Beyond MNIST
MNIST is simply the smallest playground where we could iterate quickly on curriculum mechanics, replay seeding, and evaluation plots. The environment surface that the policy sees—loss histograms, relative subset sizes, phase progress, and sample budgets—contains no MNIST-specific structure, and the actor operates purely in terms of learning-rate schedules, mixture ratios, and budget allocations. Because of that abstraction, we expect the learned curriculum policy to transfer to other supervised learning problems once new difficulty slices are supplied. Swapping MNIST for another dataset primarily involves adjusting the transforms or subset builders inside `CurriculumEnv` and pointing the configuration to a different `paths.data_path` (`curriculum_env.py:79`, `curriculum_env.py:88`, `config.yaml:75`). The same macro-accuracy reward, replay buffer, and DDPG agent continue to apply, so the policy should generalize to broader ML tasks after minimal environment adaptations.

## Implementation map
- Environment definition and per-phase SGD: `curriculum_env.py` and `curriculum.py`
- Evolutionary dataset generation: `generate_dataset.py`
- Replay buffers (uniform and prioritized): `replay_buffer.py`
- DDPG agent architecture and update logic: `rl_agent.py`
- Off-policy training loop, checkpointing, and plotting: `off_policy_train.py`
- Global hyperparameters, curriculum budgets, and paths: `config.yaml`

Together these components let us reason about curriculum composition as a reinforcement learning problem: macro accuracy keeps every subset visible, the action space manipulates the proportions and training intensity directly, and the evolutionary warm start ensures the off-policy learner sees a rich variety of strategies before fine-tuning its own.
